# V1.4 Training Plan

**Date:** 2026-02-07
**Status:** Proposed
**Author:** Training review of V1.0–V1.3 chronicle

---

## Progress to Date

| Version | Overall F1 | Tier 1 Recall | Best Epoch | Key Intervention |
|---------|-----------|---------------|------------|------------------|
| V1.0 | 0.904 | 0.722 | 5 | Baseline |
| V1.1 | 0.9005 | 0.7705 | 4 | Tier weights 3x + oversampling 3x |
| V1.2 | 0.9005 | **0.8409** | 3 | Backbone freeze after epoch 4 |
| V1.3 | **0.9071** | 0.8226 | 3 | Freeze after epoch 3 + progressive weight reduction |

Overall F1 has plateaued around 0.90 (+0.003 over 3 iterations). Tier 1 recall peaked at 0.8409 (V1.2), still 14 points short of the 0.98 target, and V1.3 actually regressed on this metric.

---

## Diagnosis

### The bottleneck is data, not training procedure

The Phase 4 entity frequency audit answered the fundamental question:

| Entity Type | Training Count | F1 |
|-------------|---------------|------|
| DATE | 169,799 | 0.960 |
| PERSON | 163,418 | 0.951 |
| BANK_ACCOUNT | 1,001 | 0.791 |
| PASSPORT_NUMBER | 526 | 0.469 |

There is a 323x frequency imbalance between the most and least common entities, and a near-linear relationship between log(entity_count) and F1. No amount of loss weighting, oversampling, or freeze scheduling will get passport recall to 0.98 from 526 examples. This has been demonstrated — 3x oversampling + 3x tier weights (9x effective amplification) bought +12 points of Tier 1 recall total across two versions, then started trading off against other tiers.

### The training spike is real but irrelevant

V1.1–V1.3 invested heavily in solving the training spike (loss explosion at epoch 4–5). V1.3 proved definitively that it originates in the head components (CharCNN, GatingFusion, or CRF), not the backbone. But the spike occurs after epoch 3, and epoch 3 is always the best checkpoint anyway. We have been debugging a failure mode that occurs in epochs we don't use.

### Diminishing returns on training dynamics

The last 3 versions explored tier weights, oversampling, freeze schedules, and progressive weight reduction. Total F1 improvement across all three: +0.003. The training dynamics axis is in the flat part of the diminishing returns curve.

## Plan

Four tracks, ordered by leverage.

### Track 1: Synthetic Data for Rare Tier 1 Entities (high leverage)

**Problem:** 5 of 6 Tier 1 entities have fewer than 5,000 training examples. Passport number has 526.

**Intervention:** Generate 10K–20K synthetic examples per underrepresented Tier 1 entity type.

**Approach:**

1. **Template extraction.** Pull real patterns from existing training data — how SSNs, passport numbers, credit cards, etc. appear in context. Catalog surrounding sentence structures and formatting variations.

2. **LLM-assisted context generation.** Use a strong LLM to generate diverse natural-language contexts containing synthetic PII values (random but format-valid). This produces novel sentence structures with correct entity annotations at essentially zero labeling cost.

3. **Validation pass.** Run generated examples through the existing V1.3 model. Prioritize hard examples where V1.3 fails — these are the ones the model needs to learn from.

4. **Integration.** Add synthetic data to the training set with a mixing ratio (e.g., 80% real + 20% synthetic) to avoid distribution shift. Validation and test sets remain entirely real data.

**Why not more oversampling:** Oversampling duplicates the same 526 passport examples. The model has already memorized them. It needs novel contexts and formatting variations.

**Expected impact:** Based on the frequency-to-performance correlation in Phase 4, increasing passport examples from 526 to 10K+ should move its F1 from 0.469 toward the 0.80+ range that entities with comparable counts achieve. Similar gains expected for bank account, tax ID, and other underrepresented Tier 1 types.

### Track 2: Training Simplification (stop at epoch 3)

The evidence is unambiguous: across V1.2 and V1.3, epoch 3 is the best checkpoint. Head-only training from epochs 4–10 in V1.3 never recovered to epoch 3 quality (peaked at F1=0.888 vs epoch 3's 0.908) and consumed 14 of 20 hours of H100 time producing nothing usable.

**V1.4 training config:**

| Parameter | V1.3 | V1.4 | Rationale |
|-----------|------|------|-----------|
| Total epochs | 10 | **3** | Epoch 3 is always best; eliminates spike entirely |
| Backbone freeze | After epoch 3 | **None** | Moot — training ends before it matters |
| Tier weights | Progressive (3x→2x) | **Constant 3x/2x/1.5x/1x** | V1.2's constant weights produced best Tier 1 recall (0.8409 vs V1.3's 0.8226); accumulation concern doesn't apply with only 3 epochs |
| Backbone LR | 1e-5 | 1e-5 | Unchanged |
| Head LR | 1e-3 | 1e-3 | Unchanged |
| LR schedule | Cosine | Cosine | Unchanged |
| Warmup | 500 steps | 500 steps | Unchanged |

**Benefits:** Training time drops from ~20 hours to ~6 hours on H100, enabling faster iteration on what actually matters — data composition and quality.

### Track 3: Head Spike Diagnostic (low cost)

Now that we know the spike is a head problem, one targeted experiment to identify which component. This is worth understanding even though it doesn't affect the epoch 3 checkpoint, because it may matter when scaling the backbone or training on more data.

1. **Per-component gradient norm logging.** Track `grad_norm_charcnn`, `grad_norm_gating`, `grad_norm_crf` separately at each training step.

2. **Short diagnostic run.** 5 epochs, backbone frozen after epoch 3 (same as V1.3). Inspect which component's gradient norm explodes at the spike point.

3. **Targeted gradient clipping.** Apply `max_grad_norm=1.0` to the offending component only.

### Track 4: Prune Zero-Occurrence Entity Types

16 entity types in the taxonomy have zero training examples: NATIONALITY, ETHNICITY, RELIGION, MARITAL_STATUS, MEDICAL_RECORD, STUDENT_ID, DEVICE_ID, CRYPTO_WALLET, INSURANCE_NUMBER, SALARY, CRIMINAL_RECORD, POLITICAL_AFFILIATION, SEXUAL_ORIENTATION, HEALTH_CONDITION, GENETIC_DATA, TRADE_UNION.

These are dead labels — the model cannot learn them. Removing them reduces the BIO label space from 89 to 57, which simplifies the CRF's partition function (fewer spurious transition paths to marginalize over).

**Recommendation:** Remove for V1.4. Re-add only when data is sourced for them.

## Defense

### Why data over architecture/hyperparameter changes

The last 3 versions explored training dynamics (tier weights, oversampling, freeze schedules, progressive weights). The F1 improvement over those 3 versions: +0.003. Meanwhile, the Phase 4 audit showed a near-linear relationship between log(entity_count) and F1. Entities with 10K+ examples routinely exceed F1=0.93. The data axis has a clear, demonstrated gradient with room to climb. The training dynamics axis is flat.

### Why 3 epochs instead of 10 with freeze

Every experiment since V1.2 confirms epoch 3 is optimal. The extra 7 epochs of head-only training in V1.3 consumed 14 of 20 hours of H100 time and produced a checkpoint inferior to epoch 3. With 3-epoch training, we can iterate 3x faster on data composition experiments.

### Why full tier weights (V1.2 strategy) instead of progressive (V1.3)

V1.2: constant 3x weights → Tier 1 recall 0.8409. V1.3: reduced to 2x after epoch 2 → Tier 1 recall 0.8226. The progressive reduction was motivated by preventing gradient accumulation damage over many epochs. With only 3 epochs, that concern evaporates. Keep the stronger signal.

### Why not scale the backbone yet

Moving to DeBERTa-v3-small (44M) or base (86M) is a reasonable future step, but the current model isn't capacity-limited. It achieves F1 > 0.93 on entities with adequate data. The model has capacity to learn Tier 1 entities — it hasn't seen enough of them. Scaling the backbone before fixing the data treats the wrong bottleneck and adds inference latency, working against the <50ms production constraint.

### What this plan does NOT do (and why)

- **More freeze schedule variants** — diminishing returns, moot with 3-epoch training.
- **CRF architecture changes** — the CRF works well; the head spike is worth diagnosing but not redesigning around.
- **Ensemble methods** — adds inference latency, contradicts <50ms production constraint.
- **Curriculum learning** — complexity not justified when the primary signal (more data for rare entities) hasn't been tried.

## Expected V1.4 Outcomes

| Metric | V1.3 (current best) | V1.4 (projected) |
|--------|---------------------|-------------------|
| Overall F1 | 0.9071 | 0.91–0.93 |
| Tier 1 Recall | 0.8226 | 0.88–0.92 |
| Tier 2 Recall | 0.9452 | 0.94–0.96 |
| Training time (H100) | 20 hours | ~6 hours |

The remaining gap from projected 0.90 to the 0.98 Tier 1 target would then point toward backbone scaling or domain-specific pretraining as the next lever — but only after the data bottleneck has been addressed.

---

## Execution Order

1. Generate synthetic Tier 1 data (Track 1) — highest leverage, do first
2. Prune zero-occurrence labels (Track 4) — quick, unblocks cleaner training
3. Train V1.4 with simplified config (Track 2) — 3 epochs, constant tier weights, synthetic data mixed in
4. Head spike diagnostic (Track 3) — run in parallel if compute is available, otherwise after V1.4 training
